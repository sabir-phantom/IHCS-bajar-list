2. How one ingredient gets its number

Every row on the forms goes down this path. The first rule that applies wins — the ones below it are never consulted.

Worth knowing: rule 2 overrides rule 3. If a row carries a saved package value, editing the recipe changes nothing for that row — this is the easiest thing in the whole system to be caught out by.

3. How the total is split

A row's total is divided between separate pieces of paper. The parts always add back to the total, so nothing is bought twice.

The order matters: the packet's share leaves first, and the cooks are split out of what remains. So a packet-only dish given to a cook puts nothing on that cook's page — all of it is already on the packet sheets.

4. What prints

Three extra sheets appear when there are packet guests. The cooks' pages are always last, so they can be torn off the back and handed over.
